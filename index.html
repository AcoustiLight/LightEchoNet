Overview of the LightEchoNet teacher–student architecture with two‑stage distillation flow.
Abstract

We present LightEchoNet, a lightweight audio classification framework designed for resource-constrained devices. Its compact CNN student provides strong standalone performance with low parameter and computational costs. The Inner Block is structurally aligned with the student and uses FusionConv blocks to produce compatible intermediate representations without additional projection modules. The proposed staged distillation strategy combines normalized Huber-based feature matching and temperature-scaled logit supervision from the Inner Block with complementary output-level guidance from the Outer Block.

The final student achieves 60.24% accuracy on TAU using the official 25% development-training subset, 93.4% on ESC-50, and 94.43% on UrbanSound8K. For a one-second audio clip, the model requires only 31.2K parameters, 28.6 MMACs, and 62.5 kB of storage in float16 format.

Motivation

Deploying audio classification models on mobile, wearable, and embedded devices is challenging because these platforms have strict limits on memory, computation, storage, and power. Although large pretrained models achieve strong performance, they are often too expensive for practical deployment. Compact models are more efficient, but reducing their size can weaken their ability to learn robust audio representations, especially under background noise, acoustic variability, and recording-device mismatch.

Motivational Points

  • Accuracy–efficiency trade-off: Lightweight models must remain accurate while using very few parameters and operations.
  • Limitations of conventional distillation: Output-level knowledge distillation does not fully transfer the teacher's intermediate representations.
  • Architectural mismatch: Direct feature matching is difficult when the teacher and student have different structures and feature dimensions.
  • No extra deployment cost: The student should benefit from powerful teachers during training without increasing its inference-time complexity.
  • Complementary supervision: Combining a structurally aligned inner teacher with a strong outer teacher can provide both feature-level and output-level guidance.
Architecture Details
Figure 1: Shared LE architecture for student and inner teacher.

Shared Student–Inner-Teacher Backbone

Figure 1 presents the structurally aligned Light Echo architectures used by the compact student and the inner teacher. Both networks follow the same stage organization and feature dimensions, containing seven LE Blocks arranged as 3+1+3 blocks and nine T–S Block positions in total. This shared structure allows their intermediate features to be compared directly without additional projection layers.

A T–S Block is a shared architectural position whose implementation depends on the network:

  • In the student, it is implemented as a standard 3×3 convolution.
  • In the inner teacher, it is replaced by the more expressive FusionBlock, also referred to as FusionConv. As detailed in Figure 2, this block processes features through parallel 3×3, 5×5, 7×7, and 3×1 convolutional branches, followed by Batch Normalization, concatenation, and a 1×1 fusion convolution.

Seven of the nine T–S Blocks appear inside the seven LE Blocks, while the remaining two are located in the convolutional stem. Each LE Block contains two branches. The transformation branch applies Batch Normalization, a 1×1 pointwise convolution, LeakyReLU, another Batch Normalization layer, a depthwise T–S operation, and a second LeakyReLU. The projection branch applies a pointwise convolution directly to the input, and the outputs of the two branches are added through a residual connection. ResNorm is used between the main stages, while AttnGAP, Batch Normalization, dropout, and a linear layer form the final classification head.

Figure 2: Staged distillation pipeline with inner and outer teachers.

Staged Inner–Outer Knowledge Distillation

Figure 2 illustrates the proposed staged distillation pipeline. First, the structurally aligned inner teacher transfers both intermediate representations and output-level knowledge to the student. Because the two networks have matching stages and feature dimensions, features from four approximately evenly spaced layers can be matched directly. The student is then refined using an outer teacher, which can be either a single high-performance model or an ensemble of teachers.

The framework therefore combines three forms of supervision:

  • Label supervision, using cross-entropy with the ground-truth labels.
  • Inner distillation, consisting of temperature-scaled logit distillation and normalized Huber-based feature matching.
  • Outer distillation, using temperature-scaled output probabilities from a single teacher or the average prediction of an ensemble.

Let \(z_s\), \(z_i\), and \(z_o\) denote the logits of the student, inner teacher, and a single outer teacher, respectively. The outer-teacher target is:

\[ p_{o}^{T} = \begin{cases} \operatorname{softmax}\!\left(\dfrac{z_o}{T}\right), & \text{single model}, \\[8pt] \dfrac{1}{K}\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K} \operatorname{softmax}\!\left(\dfrac{z_k}{T}\right), & \text{ensemble}. \end{cases} \]

The distillation distributions are defined as:

\[ p_{t}^{T} = \begin{cases} \operatorname{softmax}\!\left(\dfrac{z_i}{T}\right), & \text{inner KD}, \\[8pt] p_o^T, & \text{outer KD}, \end{cases} \qquad p_s^T = \operatorname{softmax}\!\left(\dfrac{z_s}{T}\right). \]

For feature-level inner distillation, student and inner-teacher features at layer \(l\) are \(\ell_2\)-normalized:

\[ \widehat{F}_s^{(l)} = \frac{F_s^{(l)}} {\left\lVert F_s^{(l)} \right\rVert_2+\varepsilon}, \qquad \widehat{F}_i^{(l)} = \frac{F_i^{(l)}} {\left\lVert F_i^{(l)} \right\rVert_2+\varepsilon}. \]

The feature-matching loss over the aligned layer set \(S\) is:

\[ \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{feat}} = \frac{1}{|S|} \sum_{l\in S} \frac{1}{N_l} \sum_{j=1}^{N_l} \rho_{\delta} \left( \widehat{f}_{s,j}^{(l)} - \operatorname{sg} \left( \widehat{f}_{i,j}^{(l)} \right) \right), \]

where \(\operatorname{sg}(\cdot)\) stops gradients through the teacher and \(\delta = 1\). The Huber function is:

\[ \rho_{\delta}(u) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{1}{2}u^2, & |u|\leq\delta, \\[8pt] \delta\left(|u|-\dfrac{\delta}{2}\right), & \text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \]

The complete inner-distillation objective is:

\[ \mathcal{L} = (1-\alpha)\, \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}(z_s,y) + \alpha T^2\, \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KL}} \left( p_t^T \,\Vert\, p_s^T \right) + \beta\, \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{feat}}. \]

Here, \(\alpha\) balances ground-truth and logit supervision, while \(\beta\) controls feature matching. During outer-teacher refinement, supervision is provided through cross-entropy and output-level KL distillation, without inner feature matching. After training, both teachers are discarded, so the final deployed model retains exactly the student's original architecture and inference cost.

Generalization to Compact ResNet-20 (TAU)

To examine whether the proposed structurally aligned inner–outer knowledge-distillation framework is specific to the lightweight backbone introduced in the main experiments, we additionally instantiate the same framework on a substantially different architecture: a compact ResNet-20. This experiment is designed as a controlled backbone-generalization study. The residual backbone is changed, whereas the TAU data protocol, staged distillation procedure, feature-matching formulation, and outer-teacher setting are kept consistent with the main framework.

The objective is therefore not to introduce a second task-specific architecture, but to determine whether the central principle of the proposed method—pairing lightweight student operators with structurally compatible higher-capacity inner-teacher operators—remains effective within a conventional residual network.

Compact ResNet-20 Student

We retain the complete depth and residual organization of ResNet-20 rather than obtaining a smaller model by deleting residual blocks. The network is made lightweight primarily by reducing its channel width and limiting the computational cost of the input stem. Specifically, the three residual stages use channel widths

[8, 16, 32]

which corresponds to a half-width version of the conventional [16, 32, 64] ResNet-20 channel schedule. A 3×3 stride-2 convolution is used as the input stem, followed by Batch Normalization and ReLU, without an additional max-pooling layer. Early spatial reduction lowers the cost of processing the 256×64 TAU Mel representation while preserving the complete residual hierarchy.

Each of the three stages contains three BasicBlocks, giving the following backbone:

3×3 Stem (stride 2)
→ 3 × BasicBlock(8)
→ 3 × BasicBlock(16)
→ 3 × BasicBlock(32)
→ Global Average Pooling
→ 10-class Linear Classifier

Spatial downsampling and channel expansion occur only in the first BasicBlocks of Stages 2 and 3. Thus, the model preserves the defining three-stage, nine-BasicBlock organization of ResNet-20 while using a substantially narrower feature hierarchy.

Nine Structurally Aligned T–S Positions

ResNet-20 contains exactly nine BasicBlocks, which provides a natural mapping to the nine teacher–student positions used by our framework. In every BasicBlock, we designate the second residual operator (Conv2) as the aligned T–S position:

T–S₁,₂,₃ : Conv2 of the three Stage-1 BasicBlocks
T–S₄,₅,₆ : Conv2 of the three Stage-2 BasicBlocks
T–S₇,₈,₉ : Conv2 of the three Stage-3 BasicBlocks

This choice is important because the first convolution of a transition block remains responsible for any required stride or channel change, whereas every selected Conv2 operator is stride-1 and channel preserving. Consequently, each T–S location satisfies

Cl × Hl × Wl → Cl × Hl × Wl

and can therefore host either a higher-capacity FusionBlock or its corresponding lightweight StudentBlock without changing the surrounding residual shortcut, tensor shape, stage-transition operation, or classifier. This also prevents stride and channel changes from becoming additional variables in the comparison between block families.

Structurally Aligned Inner Teacher

The compact ResNet-20 student and its inner teacher share the same global architecture. The input stem, stage organization, feature dimensions, residual connections, downsampling operations, global pooling layer, and classifier are identical. The two networks differ only at the nine predefined T–S locations.

Inner Teacher: FusionBlock   ↔   Student: StudentBlock

At each aligned position, the inner teacher employs a more expressive FusionBlock, whereas the student uses the corresponding lightweight StudentBlock. Both implementations preserve the external tensor interface of the original ResNet operator. Hence, the teacher is made richer locally without destroying its structural correspondence with the compact student.

This design is central to the proposed distillation strategy. Unlike feature distillation between unrelated architectures, corresponding inner-teacher and student representations already have compatible spatial and channel dimensions. Intermediate supervision can therefore be applied directly without introducing learned feature adapters merely to reconcile architectural mismatch.

Evaluated FusionBlock–StudentBlock Families

To further determine whether the framework depends on one particular FusionConv formulation, we evaluate three additional aligned teacher–student operator families together with the conventional FusionConv/3×3 reference pair. The surrounding ResNet-20 scaffold is unchanged in every case.

In the Windowed Transformer configuration, the inner teacher combines local positional encoding with 7×7 windowed multi-head self-attention and a gated MixFFN. The corresponding student retains the same local content-adaptive formulation but reduces its capacity through Lite W-MHSA with one or two attention heads and a Lite MixFFN with an MLP ratio of 2.

In the Large-Receptive-Field FusionConv configuration, the inner teacher extracts complementary context using parallel 5×5, 7×7, 9×9, and asymmetric 5×1 convolutional branches. Their outputs are normalized, concatenated, and combined through a 1×1 fusion convolution. The corresponding student replaces this multi-branch representation with a single 5×5 convolution, retaining an enlarged local receptive field with a substantially simpler implementation.

Finally, the Diverse Fusion configuration uses heterogeneous convolutional and pooling paths in the inner teacher. The corresponding student adopts 3×3, 1×1, and identity branches during training. These branches are subsequently merged by structural reparameterization into a single 3×3 convolution for deployment. Because every selected T–S location satisfies stride = 1 and Cin = Cout, the identity branch is valid at all nine positions.

Intermediate Feature Distillation

Although the backbone contains nine aligned T–S operators, feature loss is deliberately not imposed after every replacement block. Following the main framework, we retain four intermediate supervision positions to distribute feature guidance across network depth without introducing a highly redundant loss after every BasicBlock.

The selected feature set is

𝒮 = { FB2, FB4, FB6, FB9 },   |𝒮| = 4

These checkpoints provide supervision at progressively deeper residual representations and include the output of the final BasicBlock. Because every FusionBlock and StudentBlock preserves the dimensions of its assigned position, the corresponding inner-teacher and student features remain directly compatible.

The same ℓ2-normalized Huber feature loss used in the main architecture can therefore be applied without additional projection modules:

\[ \widehat{F}_{s}^{(l)} = \frac{F_s^{(l)}} {\left\lVert F_s^{(l)} \right\rVert_2+\varepsilon}, \qquad \widehat{F}_{i}^{(l)} = \frac{F_i^{(l)}} {\left\lVert F_i^{(l)} \right\rVert_2+\varepsilon}. \]

Inner–Outer Distillation Protocol

To separate backbone generalization from changes in the optimization procedure, the compact ResNet experiment follows the same staged inner–outer distillation strategy as the primary system.

During the first student-training stage, the structurally aligned ResNet inner teacher provides both intermediate feature supervision and temperature-scaled logit supervision. The same inner-distillation coefficients are retained:

α = 0.6,   β = 0.8,   T = 5

The resulting compact student is subsequently refined through the output-level outer-teacher stage, using

α = 0.95,   β = 0,   T = 8.

The outer teacher operates only on output distributions and therefore does not need to share the ResNet architecture or intermediate feature dimensions. For the controlled TAU comparison, the outer-teacher configuration is kept identical across all four ResNet variants.

ResNet Inner Teacher → Feature + Logit KD → Compact ResNet-20 Student → Outer-Teacher Logit KD

Across all variants, the ResNet-20 scaffold, channel widths, nine T–S locations, four feature-loss positions, TAU protocol, optimization procedure, and outer-teacher setting are fixed. Only the internal FusionBlock–StudentBlock family is changed. This controlled design isolates the effect of the aligned operator pair from differences in network depth, downsampling, feature dimensions, or distillation strategy.

Analysis

The four configurations exhibit different standalone behavior and different responses to distillation, rather than following an artificially uniform gain pattern. The conventional 3×3 student provides the reference residual implementation, improving from 54.5% to 57.3% after inner distillation and to 59.2% after the complete inner–outer pipeline.

The Windowed Transformer student has slightly lower standalone accuracy, which is consistent with the weaker convolutional inductive bias of a small attention-based operator when trained directly on a comparatively limited acoustic-scene dataset. However, it receives the largest overall improvement from teacher supervision, increasing from 54.2% to 59.6%. This behavior is consistent with the role of distillation as an additional optimization and representation-learning signal for a compact attention module.

The Large-RF student performs more strongly from scratch and reaches 59.9% after the full pipeline. Its 5×5 student operator provides a wider local spectro-temporal receptive field, while the inner teacher supplies complementary representations from 5×5, 7×7, 9×9, and asymmetric 5×1 branches. The result therefore reflects a balance between explicit multi-scale context in the teacher and a substantially simpler single-branch student.

Among the evaluated configurations, Diverse Fusion provides the strongest final result. Its student benefits from complementary 3×3, 1×1, and identity paths during training and reaches 58.5% after inner distillation. The subsequent outer-teacher stage increases accuracy to 60.1%. Importantly, the training-time branches do not increase deployment complexity because structural reparameterization collapses them into a single 3×3 convolution for inference.

The magnitude of the outer-KD improvement also decreases naturally for the strongest inner-distilled convolutional students. The conventional, Transformer, Large-RF, and Diverse variants receive additional gains of approximately 1.9, 2.1, 1.8, and 1.6 percentage points, respectively. This is consistent with diminishing returns as the student approaches a stronger representation before the final distillation stage.

More importantly, the comparison does not depend on a single FusionBlock design. All four operator families benefit from the same structurally aligned training principle despite employing substantially different internal mechanisms, including conventional convolution, local self-attention, enlarged receptive fields, and reparameterizable multi-branch processing.

Taken together, this experiment supports a deliberately bounded but meaningful generalization claim: the proposed inner–outer distillation framework is not restricted to the original LightEcho backbone. When higher-capacity teacher operators and lightweight student operators are placed at shape-compatible locations, the same structural-alignment principle can also be instantiated within a conventional ResNet-20 backbone. The experiment therefore provides evidence of transfer across both backbone design and aligned operator family without requiring additional feature-projection modules or changing the student's inference-time structure.
